1. Introduction and Scope

This Privacy Policy explains how AST Consulting ("we", "us", "our") collects, uses, processes, stores, and protects information when you access or use Odin CMS (the "Service"), accessible via our website at https://www.odincms.com, our hosted cloud platform, and self-hosted instances connected to optional cloud services.

Odin CMS is an enterprise content management and AI-assisted editorial operating system engineered specifically for newsrooms, digital dailies, broadcast TV digital desks, vernacular publishers, and media networks. To deliver seamless multi-channel distribution and audience performance measurement, Odin CMS provides native integrations with third-party service providers, most notably Google LLC ("Google") APIs including YouTube, Google Analytics 4 (GA4), and Google Search Console.

This Policy governs all users of Odin CMS, including editors, journalists, administrators, video producers, and contributors. Please read this Privacy Policy alongside our Terms of Service. Complete step-by-step Data Deletion and Account Disconnection Instructions are set out in Section 10 below.

2. Information Collected During Account Registration & CMS Usage

When you register, access, or operate within an Odin CMS workspace, we collect the following categories of information:

  • Identity and Contact Data: User full name, professional email address, organization name, job title, and role within your publication's workspace.
  • Authentication Credentials: Securely salted and hashed passwords (using bcrypt algorithms) for direct email/password sign-in, or unique third-party identity references (such as Google OAuth Subject IDs) for Single Sign-On.
  • Editorial and Publication Content: Story text, article drafts, headlines, summaries, custom taxonomy, author bylines, uploaded media assets (images, graphics, video files, audio tracks), editorial assignments, publication dates, and revision histories created inside the CMS editor.
  • System Logs & Audit Trails: IP addresses, browser user-agents, session identifiers, timestamps of logins, article modifications, publishing events, and API interactions. Audit logs are maintained to protect newsroom data integrity, detect unauthorized access, and satisfy editorial accountability requirements.

3. Google Account Information Accessed Through OAuth

Odin CMS allows users to sign in using Google OAuth (OpenID Connect) and to connect their organization's Google services from the Settings → Integrations screen.

When you authorize Odin CMS using Google Sign-In, we request the standard OpenID scopes (openid, email, profile) and access the following information:

  • Google User ID (sub): A unique opaque numeric identifier provided by Google to verify your identity.
  • Email Address: Your primary Google account email, used to identify your account and send essential workspace notifications.
  • Display Name: Your given name and family name as registered in your Google profile.
  • Profile Picture URL: Your public Google profile avatar URL, used solely to display your user profile picture within the Odin CMS dashboard.

What We Do Not Collect: We never access or store your Google account password, Google Drive files, personal contacts, personal Gmail messages, or search history.

4. YouTube Channel Information and Video Metadata

When a media organization connects a YouTube channel to Odin CMS via the YouTube integration, we request the read-only scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly. Through this scope, Odin CMS accesses:

  • Channel Profile Information: Channel ID, channel title, description, custom vanity URL, channel avatar/thumbnail URLs, banner image, and creation date.
  • Channel Statistics: Total subscriber count, total public video count, and aggregate view counts.
  • Video Catalog Metadata: Lists of videos published on the connected channel, including video IDs, titles, descriptions, publication timestamps, category IDs, tags, video duration, and current privacy status (public, unlisted, private).
  • Playlists: Playlist IDs, playlist titles, and video assignments within the connected channel.

Purpose of Processing: This data is accessed solely to display the connected channel within Odin CMS's YouTube Studio module, allowing newsroom producers to monitor channel health, select playlists for distribution, and reference published video assets directly inside the story editor.

5. YouTube Video Upload, Editing, & Management Permissions

To enable newsrooms to publish video packages, breaking news clips, and vertical Shorts directly from the Odin CMS Video Studio to YouTube, Odin CMS requests the following management scopes:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.force-ssl

These scopes grant Odin CMS the permissions necessary to perform user-initiated publishing and management actions:

  • Resumable Video Uploads (videos.insert): Transmitting video files generated or edited in Odin CMS to your connected YouTube channel using Google's secure resumable upload protocol.
  • Editorial Metadata Updates (videos.update): Setting or updating video titles, descriptions, editorial tags, category mappings, and language metadata per your newsroom's editorial instructions.
  • Custom Thumbnail Publication (thumbnails.set): Uploading custom cover cards and high-resolution thumbnail images for your uploaded videos.
  • Playlist Organization (playlistItems.insert / playlistItems.delete): Adding newly uploaded videos to appropriate editorial topic playlists (e.g., "Breaking News", "Interviews", "Special Reports").
  • Native Scheduling & Privacy: Setting the video privacy state (public, unlisted, or private) and configuring automated scheduled publication times (status.publishAt) according to the editorial release calendar.
  • AI Disclosure Compliance: Setting the status.containsSyntheticMedia flag when videos contain AI-generated footage or synthetic audio, in full compliance with YouTube community guidelines.
Operational Boundaries of YouTube Permissions

Odin CMS operates strictly under your explicit direction. Odin CMS never deletes your YouTube channel, never modifies or deletes videos not managed through our workspace, never posts comments on behalf of your channel, and never likes or subscribes to third-party channels.

6. YouTube Analytics Data Accessed and Displayed

When authorized by the publisher, Odin CMS uses the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/yt-analytics.readonly to access aggregated channel and video analytics via the YouTube Analytics Reporting API (reports.query).

Metrics accessed and displayed in Odin CMS include:

  • Core Engagement: Views, estimated minutes watched, average view duration, likes, comments, and shares.
  • Subscriber Dynamics: Subscribers gained, subscribers lost, and net subscriber growth over selected time periods.
  • Audience Acquisition: Traffic source breakdown (e.g., YouTube search, suggested videos, external embeds, direct).
  • Geographic Distribution: Country-level viewership distribution for editorial reach assessment.
  • Content Leaderboards: Top-performing videos and daily trend timelines.

Storage & Processing: YouTube Analytics metrics are retrieved on demand and cached in an in-memory Redis layer with short Time-To-Live (TTL) values (typically 15 to 30 minutes) to minimize Google API quota consumption and ensure fast dashboard response. Historical aggregate channel performance metrics (such as daily view and subscriber growth) are maintained in database snapshot collections governed by automated MongoDB TTL expiration indexes (14 days for hourly snapshots, up to 400–800 days for daily historical trend records). Odin CMS does not store individual end-user viewer identities or personally identifiable viewer data.

7. Google Analytics 4 & Google Search Console Data

Newsrooms rely on audience feedback and organic search visibility to shape their editorial agenda. Odin CMS integrates with Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console to bring these insights directly into the content workflow:

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) — https://www.googleapis.com/auth/analytics.readonly

When you connect Google Analytics 4, Odin CMS accesses:

  • Account & Property Structure: The list of GA4 accounts and web properties accessible by your credentials, enabling you to select the correct property for each publication domain.
  • Realtime Metrics: Concurrent active users on your news portal, active screen/page views, and top trending active stories.
  • Historical Editorial Metrics: Total users, new users, sessions, pageviews, engagement rate, average session duration, and traffic acquisition channels (organic, direct, referral, social).
  • Story Performance: Page path, article titles, publication dates, and author-level traffic aggregations.

Google Search Console — https://www.googleapis.com/auth/webmasters.readonly

When you connect Google Search Console, Odin CMS accesses:

  • Verified Site Entry: The list of verified web domains and URL prefixes in your Search Console account to map to your Odin CMS publication.
  • Search Performance Metrics: Total organic clicks, impressions, average click-through rate (CTR), and average ranking position.
  • Top Queries & Landing Pages: Leading search keywords bringing organic readers to your publication, top indexed URLs, and search appearance classifications (web, news, discover, video, image).

Both GA4 and Search Console metrics are queried strictly in read-only mode, displayed within the editor and analytics tabs, and temporarily cached in Redis to reduce redundant API calls.

8. OAuth Access Tokens and Refresh Tokens

Why Tokens Are Needed

Google OAuth access tokens have a lifespan of exactly 60 minutes (3,600 seconds). To maintain continuous functionality—such as background scheduled video publishing, automated draft synchronization, and updated analytics dashboards—Odin CMS requests offline access (access_type: 'offline'). This provides a cryptographically secure refresh token that allows our backend to transparently obtain fresh access tokens without requiring newsroom staff to repeatedly re-authenticate through Google.

How Tokens Are Stored

OAuth tokens are stored exclusively in our isolated production MongoDB database within the integrations collection. Fields stored include accessToken, refreshToken, expiresAt, scope, and tokenType.

  • Backend-Only Isolation: Tokens reside exclusively on private server-side infrastructure and are never transmitted to user browsers, client-side scripts, or external frontends.
  • API Sanitization: All API endpoints returning integration status explicitly strip credentials using MongoDB query projections (.select('-credentials')).
  • No Token Logging: OAuth tokens, secrets, and authorization codes are strictly redacted from application log files and exception traces.

Security Measures Protecting Tokens

Token storage is protected by strict infrastructure controls: mandatory TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3 encryption in transit, storage volume-level AES-256 encryption at rest across database clusters, private Virtual Private Cloud (VPC-only) database network isolation, and least-privilege role-based access controls. Tokens are not exposed to the browser and are never transmitted to client-side code.

How Users Can Revoke Access

You can revoke Odin CMS's access to your Google account at any time via two distinct methods:

  1. In-App Disconnection: Navigate to Settings → Integrations in your Odin CMS workspace, locate the connected Google service (YouTube, Google Analytics, or Search Console), and click Disconnect. Our backend will immediately wipe the stored credentials and proactively notify Google's authorization server to revoke the token grant.
  2. Google Account Permissions Dashboard: You can independently revoke access at any time directly through Google by visiting Google Account Security → Third-party apps with account access. Once revoked in Google, Odin CMS will be permanently unable to access your Google account until re-authorized.

We process collected data exclusively for the following legitimate business purposes:

  • Delivering Core CMS Functionality: Authenticating users, managing multi-author editorial workflows, rendering article layouts, scheduling publication, and distributing content across digital channels.
  • Executing Requested Integrations: Uploading videos to YouTube, applying metadata, setting custom thumbnails, querying GA4 audience statistics, and retrieving Google Search Console performance data upon your command.

Odin CMS does not use your editorial drafts, proprietary news content, or Google user data to train, retrain, or fine-tune third-party public foundation AI models (such as those from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google). Your content remains sovereign to your newsroom.

10. Data Storage, Retention, and Deletion Instructions

We adhere to strict data minimization principles under the Google API Services User Data Policy, EU/UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and India's DPDP Act 2023. You maintain full sovereignty over your data and can disconnect integrations or request permanent deletion at any time.

10.1 Retention Periods

  • Connected Google Integrations: OAuth tokens and channel/property configuration metadata are retained only while the integration remains in connected status. Upon disconnection, tokens and configuration metadata are wiped immediately.
  • Volatile In-Memory Caching (Redis): Query responses and operational caches fetched from Google APIs are held temporarily in Redis with strictly defined Time-To-Live (TTL) expirations:
    • Video lists, video details, and channel summaries: 5 to 10 minutes.
    • Topic playlists and video analytics queries: 15 to 30 minutes.
    • Comment sentiment analysis caches: 6 hours.
    • GA4 account listings and Search Console error logs: 24 hours.
    All cached entries expire and are purged automatically by Redis once their TTL elapses.
  • Historical Performance Snapshots (MongoDB): For connected YouTube channels, aggregate daily performance metrics (such as daily view counts and subscriber totals) are recorded in dedicated snapshot collections to provide historical growth curves inside YouTube Studio. These records are governed by automated MongoDB TTL indexes (14 days for hourly snapshots, up to 400–800 days for historical daily trend summaries).
  • Active Workspaces: Editorial articles, media assets, and workspace audit logs are retained for the duration of your active subscription.
  • Terminated Workspaces: Upon written account cancellation or complete data deletion request, all active customer records and stored snapshots are permanently purged within 30 calendar days.

10.2 Self-Service Disconnection (Instant Token & Cache Wipe)

Any authorized newsroom administrator or editor can disconnect Google integrations directly in the Odin CMS interface:

Automated Deletion Actions Upon Disconnection

When you click Disconnect, Odin CMS executes the following actions immediately:

  • Instant Token Erasure: The stored accessToken and refreshToken are immediately wiped from our production database.
  • Metadata Clearing: Saved property IDs, channel labels, and configuration metadata are reset to empty.
  • Cache Invalidation: Provider-specific cached analytics data in our Redis memory layer (e.g., GA4 property mappings, Search Console error logs, and YouTube Studio video cache counters) is invalidated immediately, and transient operational entries expire automatically via their short TTLs.
  • Proactive Google Revocation: Our backend issues an asynchronous HTTP call to Google's OAuth revocation endpoint (https://oauth2.googleapis.com/revoke) to inform Google's servers that the grant has been terminated.
  • Status Update: The integration status transitions to DISCONNECTED, preventing any further API calls.

13. Security Measures and Limitations

We employ robust technical and organizational security measures designed to protect information from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction:

  • Transport Layer Security: All web traffic and API communications are encrypted using modern TLS (TLS 1.2 and TLS 1.3) protocols.
  • Database Encryption: All persistent database volumes, database backups, and media archives are encrypted at rest using industry-standard AES-256 encryption.
  • Network Isolation: Production databases and caching nodes run in private Virtual Private Clouds (VPCs) without public internet access, reachable only by authenticated application services.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Granular permissions inside Odin CMS ensure users only access features and integrations authorized by workspace administrators.
  • Continuous Monitoring & Dependency Scanning: Automated security scanning tools continuously monitor dependencies for known vulnerabilities and security advisories.

Limitations: While we implement industry-standard safeguards, no method of transmission over the Internet or electronic storage can be guaranteed 100% secure. If a security breach impacting your personal data occurs, we will notify affected administrators and regulatory authorities in compliance with applicable law.

14. User Rights and Data Deletion Requests

Depending on your jurisdiction (including under the EU/UK GDPR, California Consumer Privacy Act / CPRA, and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023), you hold distinct legal rights regarding your personal information:

  • Right of Access & Portability: You may request a copy of the personal data we hold about you in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format.
  • Right to Rectification: You may correct or update inaccurate or incomplete personal information through your user profile or by contacting us.
  • Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten"): You have the right to request the deletion of your personal data and connected integration tokens.
  • Right to Restrict or Object to Processing: You may object to specific processing activities or request that we restrict processing in certain circumstances.
  • Right to Withdraw Consent: Where data processing is based on consent, you may withdraw your consent at any time without affecting the lawfulness of prior processing.

To exercise any of these rights, email us at connect@odincms.com or ashok@astconsulting.in. We respond to all verified requests within 30 days without charge.

16. Cookies and Tracking Technologies

Odin CMS uses cookies and similar session storage technologies solely to ensure platform security and remember user preferences:

  • Strictly Necessary Cookies: Secure session identifiers and CSRF tokens required to authenticate you, protect against cross-site request forgery, and keep you signed in while navigating your workspace.
